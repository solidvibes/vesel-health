Why your numbers go stale

The Polar Loop keeps everything it measures and waits for the phone to come and collect it. iOS only lets Vesel collect for a short time, and today Vesel saves nothing until it has collected everything.

What you get

Numbers from the last hour or two

Every read keeps what it got. Vesel gets more chances to read during the day, plus a long read at night while the phone charges.

What you do

Nothing, mostly

Tap Sync now or I'm up and you can leave the app at once: iOS shows a small card while the read finishes.

Stays safe

Nothing is deleted from the Loop too early

Vesel clears the Loop only after a whole read: every part came back and the connection never dropped. Anything less clears nothing.

More reads cost the band some battery. The Syncing page shows how often Vesel read, so you can see it.

Needs your answer

Four questions

1 · Read at fixed times?

The Loop never asks for a read on a schedule. You set up the "Sync Vesel" Shortcut at, say, 12:00, 16:00 and 20:00 in the Shortcuts app. Recommended: yes. Or: a one-line tip on the Syncing page instead.

2 · More reads, a little more band battery?

Maybe a read every hour or two on a normal day. Recommended: yes.

3 · Are the words OK?

Shown in the pictures below: the new Syncing rows, the reminder, the iOS card.

4 · Is the Loop's own "sit still" alert on?

Vesel can't switch it on; only the Polar app can, and opening it sends your Loop's data to Polar's cloud.

Decisions

  1. Yours: save as you go, quick things first, a long read when iOS allows.
  2. Yours: stand-up reminders, off until you turn them on.
  3. Yours: Sync now and I'm up keep reading after you leave the app.
  4. Order: last night, steps, activity, skin temperature, then heart rate and beat-to-beat, the slowest two.
  5. An empty day is only believed after a whole read with no dropped connection; Polar's software can report a drop as "no data".
  6. After a day you didn't wear the Loop, newer sleep and steps wait for one whole read; after that they come in as usual.
  7. A day counts as not worn only after two empty reads at least an hour apart. Hard to undo: no.
  8. Opening Vesel reads again if the last read didn't finish, even within 5 minutes.
  9. Reminders: silent, nothing 22:00–07:00, never on Today, dropped if older than 5 minutes.
  10. Vesel never changes the Loop's own settings.

Risks

  1. Deleting data from the Loop (a sensitive area). Shows as: a day missing on the phone. Guarded by the rules in 5–6 and tests that cut reads off on purpose.
  2. The Loop may send fewer check-ins than hoped. Shows as: few "checked in" rows. Then the Shortcut times carry the day.
  3. Heart rate with a backlog may only catch up when Vesel is open or at night. Shows as: steps fresh, heart rate later.
  4. The iOS card's progress moves per part, so a long heart-rate read can look stuck and iOS may end it. Shows as: "didn't finish" on the Syncing page; nothing is cleared.

How we'll know it works

  1. Automatic tests, including reads cut off at every step: nothing is cleared that isn't on the phone.
  2. The app on the Simulator: reading list and the new switch.
  3. Only you, on your iPhone: over a day, "checked in" rows and Today's steps no older than an hour or two.
  4. Only you: after a night charging, a "long read while charging" row that ended "done".
  5. Only you: tap Sync now, leave at once: the iOS card shows, and the read ends "done". Once more after a few days without reading.
  6. Only you: sit still until the Loop buzzes: "Time to move" on the phone.
